## The app, compared with a hospital

The class has the same needs as an outpatient department: register people once, see them on each
visit, note their progress, and follow up those who stop coming. The app was designed that way.

| In a hospital | In the class app |
|---|---|
| **Registration** at the front desk, with a patient ID (UHID) | **Register a student**: two minutes at the desk; a **roll number** such as `MS-2026-0001`, never changed and never given to anyone else |
| The **token** or wristband scanned at each visit | **My QR** on the student's phone, scanned by the coordinator on arrival and departure |
| The **OPD visit** record | A **visit**: time in, time out, who marked it |
| **Progress notes** in the case sheet, signed and dated | **Syllabus tick-off**: each item a student shows in class is ticked with the date and the coordinator's name. A tick removed by mistake leaves a trace, like a correction in a case sheet |
| A patient **lost to follow-up** | A student with no visit for 14 days becomes **Irregular**; after 30 days **Inactive** |
| The **follow-up call** and its note | **Follow-up calls**: the mentor phones, then records the outcome, the reason and what was said |
| **Discharge** only with a documented reason | A student can be marked **Left** or **Paused** only through a logged call, never by editing a field |
| **Informed consent**, and a guardian's consent for a minor | A **parent's signed consent** is required for every student under 18; the system refuses to save a minor without it |
| **Need-to-know access** to records | Students see only their own record; coordinators and the Guru see what they need to teach |
| The **audit trail** of the hospital information system | The **audit log**: every change to a student, call, tick, lesson or setting, who made it and when |
| **Equipment register** and the **duty roster** | **Instruments** (who has which mridanga, its condition) and the **Duty roster** |

## How your data, and the children's data, are protected

The same principles you follow with patient records are built into the app:

1. **Consent first.** For a child, a parent fills in and signs a paper form at the desk. The
   coordinator ticks in the app that it was received. The database itself refuses to keep a minor's
   details without it — not just the screen, so no shortcut or old app version can skip it.
2. **Minimum necessary data.** Only the area and pincode are stored, never the full address. For
   the parent's identity check, only the **type** of ID seen (Aadhaar, PAN…) is recorded, never the
   number. No health information is asked for.
3. **Confidentiality.** Each person sees only what their role needs. A student cannot open another
   student's record. Parents' details are visible only to coordinators and the Guru. Replies to an
   announcement are private. Votes in an anonymous poll are never shown, even to the Guru.
4. **Documentation you can trust.** Every tick carries the name of the person who really made it,
   written by the system, not typed. Every change is kept in the audit log.
5. **A law to respect.** India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act and Rules require verifiable
   parental consent for children. The class follows a signed paper form now.

## A word of care about health details

*Suggested practice, until the team agrees its own.*

When you log a follow-up call, the app offers reasons such as **Health**, **Studies / exams** or
**Family**, and asks **What was said**. Please write only what the next caller needs, for example
"Unwell, will return after two weeks". **Do not write a diagnosis, medicines or test results.** The
class is not a clinic, and such details do not belong in its records. Encourage parents to share
health matters (allergies, epilepsy, asthma) in person with the Guru instead.

## If you come as a parent

- Come with your child on the first day, fill in and sign the consent form, and show your ID to
  the coordinator (they note only its type).
- Tick **photo consent** only if you agree to a photo of your child on the class record.
- You may withdraw consent at any time: speak to the Guru.
- Coordinators may phone you if your child stops coming, simply to ask if all is well.

## If you serve as a coordinator

Your shifts may be short and unpredictable, like on-call duty. The app helps a coordinator pick up
where another left off:

- **Mark attendance**: scan the student's QR code, or type two letters of the name and tap
  **Check in**.
- **Who is here now**: everyone checked in. At closing time, **Check out all**.
- **A student's profile**: details, visits, calls, syllabus, all in one place, like a case summary.
- **Tick syllabus items**: tap the box when the student shows an item; **Tick with a remark** to
  add a note such as "needs a steadier tempo".
- **Calls** tab: who needs a call, grouped as **Call due**, **Call later**, **Needs the facilitator**.
  Tap a name, tap **Call** to dial, then record the outcome.
- **Duty roster**: your shifts for the next 4 weeks, with a reminder the evening before.

## If you come to learn

The mridanga is a wonderful practice for busy hands and minds. The app gives you:

- a **Metronome** and **Taal player** to practise at home at your own speed,
- **Both heads**, a drawing that lights up where each stroke falls on the drum,
- lesson videos you can slow down and loop,
- a **practice timer** and **practice log**, so even 10 minutes after a long duty counts,
- and the **Slokas** tab, a verse each day with its meaning.

Steps: [For college students](for-college-students.md) (they apply to every adult learner).

## Why trust it?

- It was planned with the class team, screen by screen, and every important decision is written down
  with its reason.
- Every change is checked before it reaches a phone, including an automatic test of hundreds of
  rules about who may see and change what.
- It is tested on a separate copy filled with made-up students. Real records are never used for
  testing.
- It is free and open: the code can be read by anyone.
