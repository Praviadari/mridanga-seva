# The app in simple words

How does the app work, and how does it keep our details safe? Here it is with everyday pictures,
and no computer words.

[← Easy guide](README.md)

---

## Your phone is a window; the register is in the office

Think of the temple office. It keeps one big **register** for the class, in a locked cupboard.

- The **app on your phone** is like a **window** to that register. It shows you your own page,
  and lets you write what you are allowed to write.
- The register itself is not on your phone. It is kept safely on a computer on the internet, which
  is always on (a "server"). So if you lose your phone, nothing in the register is lost.
- Every coordinator looks through their own window at the **same** register. That is why the
  coordinator on Monday knows what the coordinator on Saturday taught you.

## A careful clerk checks every request

At the cupboard sits a very careful **clerk**. Every time any phone asks to see or change
something, the clerk checks:

- **Who are you?** (Your email and password tell them.)
- **Are you allowed?** A student may see only their own page. A coordinator may see the students'
  pages. Only the Guru may change the syllabus or the settings.
- **Is it correct?** For example: *"A child under 18 without a parent's signed form? I will not
  write that."*

The clerk follows the rules **every time**, whichever phone asks, even an old copy of the app. This
is the most important safety idea in the app. A pharmacist checks a prescription the same way,
whoever brings it to the counter.

Some of the clerk's rules:

| The rule | Why |
|---|---|
| No child's details without a parent's signed consent | The law and our duty of care |
| A roll number never changes and is never given to anyone else | Your record stays yours for life |
| A student is marked "Left" only after a phone call is noted, with the reason | Nobody is dropped by mistake |
| Each tick on your progress carries the real name of the teacher who ticked it | Records you can trust |
| Only the Guru moves a student up a level | Promotion is the Guru's decision |
| Your notes on a sloka are for your eyes only | Privacy |
| In an anonymous poll, nobody sees what you chose | Honest answers |
| Every change is written in a logbook | If something goes wrong, we can see what happened |

## Only what is needed

The class keeps as little about you as it can:

- your **area and pincode**, not your full address;
- for a parent's ID, only **which kind** of ID was seen, never the number;
- no health details, no bank details, no money. **The app never asks for payment.**

## A practice copy and the real one

Before anything new reaches the class, it is tried on a **practice copy** of the register, filled
with **made-up students** (like a mock drill, or a practice exam). Real names are never used for
testing. Only when it works there does it go to the real one.

## How new things reach your phone

- **Small changes** (new screens, better words) arrive by themselves. The home screen shows **A new
  version is ready**. Tap **Restart now**. It takes a few seconds — like new pages slipped into your
  book.
- **Big changes** (for example, adding sound or the microphone) need a **new copy of the app** — a
  new book. The class shares a link; you install it once.
- On an iPhone or a computer, the newest version is simply there the next time you open it.

## Without internet

- Your **QR card** still shows, because a copy is kept on your phone.
- If you were signed in before, the app opens on your home screen.
- To **save** anything (a visit, a mark, a message), the phone must reach the register, so it needs
  the internet.

## Three languages

The app speaks **English**, **తెలుగు** (Telugu) and **हिन्दी** (Hindi). Change it at the bottom of
the home screen. The Telugu and Hindi words are first drafts; tell a coordinator if a word sounds wrong.

## Free, open, and made as seva

- It costs nothing to run. Every service it uses is free.
- Its "recipe" (the code) is open for anyone to read, so nothing is hidden.
- It is offered to Lord Krishna, so that the devotees who teach can give their time to the students
  and the kirtan, not to paperwork.
